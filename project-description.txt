Project Title
Dual-Spectrum Planetary Micro-Rover: Autonomous 3D Surface Mapping and Acoustic Subsurface Regolith Profiling
1. Executive Summary
Planetary exploration rovers face two critical navigational and scientific challenges: navigating unpredictable surface topography and detecting hazards buried beneath loose soil/regolith (e.g., hollow crusts, soft dust traps, or solid bedrock).
This project implements a budget-conscious (~$50 added hardware), autonomous micro-rover prototype using an MSP432 MCU (ARM Cortex-M4F) and a 2-wheel differential drive base. The system integrates a panning Time-of-Flight (ToF) LiDAR for real-time 3D topographic surface mapping alongside an Active Acoustic Impulse Spectroscopy payload—leveraging a custom 2-channel audio signal processing PCB—to perform real-time subsurface material classification.

Hardware Components & Budget
Main Microcontroller: MSP432P401R (48 MHz ARM Cortex-M4F with hardware FPU, 14-bit ADC).
Base Platform: 2-wheel differential-drive robot chassis with wheel quadrature encoders.
Acoustic Front-End: Reused UC Davis 2-Channel Sound Detector PCB (LM6134 Op-Amp, onboard gain stage, and $RC$ low-pass filter).
Impulse Actuator: 5V push-pull solenoid driven via N-channel MOSFET circuit.
Acoustic Sensor: Piezoelectric contact transducer / electret microphone mounted to chassis.
3D LiDAR Mapping: VL53L1X Time-of-Flight (ToF) distance sensor ($4\,\text{m}$ range) mounted to an SG90 micro-servo pan platform.
Estimated Project Cost: ~$45 – $60 (Well under the $100 limit).
3. Subsystem Functionality
A. Subsurface Acoustic Spectroscopy (Geological Profiling)
Impulse Trigger: The rover halts motor PWM output (eliminating mechanical vibration and electrical noise) and fires a $20\,\text{ms}$ pulse to the solenoid, delivering a physical "tap" to the ground.
Signal Conditioning: The reverberation signal passes into the UC Davis Audio PCB. The inverting op-amp stage applies gain (adjusted via $VR1$), while the $RC$ low-pass filter ($VR2 + R9 + C3$) removes unwanted high-frequency motor/ambient noise to act as an anti-aliasing filter before sampling.
DSP Pipeline (MSP432): The MSP432 samples the signal via ADC14 with Timer_A DMA ping-pong buffers. It runs an $N=512$ point Real FFT using the ARM CMSIS-DSP library (arm_cfft_f32).
Classification Logic:
High Frequency (>2 kHz) & Slow Decay: High-density surface (bedrock / concrete).
Low Frequency (<500 Hz) & Rapid Damping: Soft surface (loose soil / sand / dust trap hazard).
B. Topographic 3D Point Cloud Mapping
2D Sweep Angle: The SG90 micro-servo pans the ToF sensor continuously across a $90^\circ$ arc ($-45^\circ$ to $+45^\circ$) ahead of the rover.
Odometry Fusion: Wheel encoders track distance traveled ($x, y$) and heading angle ($\theta$). Combining encoder odometry, servo position angle ($\phi$), and distance ($r$), the MSP432 computes 3D spatial points:
$$x_{point} = x_{rover} + r \cdot \cos(\theta + \phi)$$
$$y_{point} = y_{rover} + r \cdot \sin(\theta + \phi)$$
$$z_{point} = z_{rover} + r \cdot \sin(\text{elevation angle})$$
Data Telemetry: Point coordinates $(x, y, z)$ along with soil hardness tags are streamed over UART/Bluetooth to a visualization host (Processing / Python / ROS).

5. Expected Results & Key Metrics
3D Mapping Resolution: Angular sweep resolution of $\sim 2^\circ$ yielding a dense point cloud grid within a $2\,\text{m}$ range envelope.
Spectral Distinction: Clear frequency distribution separation (>15 dB shift in peak frequency magnitude) between soft sand/rug vs. hard tile/concrete floors.
Sampling Rate: Subsecond acoustic tap-to-FFT execution (<200 ms latency thanks to CMSIS-DSP hardware acceleration).
